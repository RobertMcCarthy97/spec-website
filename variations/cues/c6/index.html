We measure the character of AI systems and verify whether they behave as their specifications say.

SPEC, the Spec & Propensity Evaluation Center, builds evaluations of AI propensities and character. Frontier labs now publish the behaviour they intend their models to have, in documents such as Claude's constitution and OpenAI's model spec. We check whether models actually live up to them, prioritising the areas that matter most for reducing catastrophic risk, and we do work that encourages the design of specifications that reduce that risk.

Current project: concentration of power

We are evaluating whether AIs will assist in government power concentration. Helpful-only government AIs could enable extreme concentrations of power, authoritarian expansion of executive power, and erosion of the rule of law. Both major specs state red lines here, yet a basic eval, The Dictatorship Eval, already exposes flaws in current models. We are building a dataset to find where models cross those lines, where their refusal boundaries sit, how easily they can be pushed across them, and what else they do besides comply or refuse.

This project is advised by Kevin Frazier, Kevin Wei, and Harvey Lederman. We are based in Berkeley and funded by Coefficient Giving.
